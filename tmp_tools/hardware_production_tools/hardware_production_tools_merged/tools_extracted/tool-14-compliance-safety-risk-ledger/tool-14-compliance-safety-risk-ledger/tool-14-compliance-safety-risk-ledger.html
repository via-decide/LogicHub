Compliance / Safety Risk Ledger

A release-control tool for hardware projects that may touch mains power, batteries, RF, cameras, medical/lab claims, robotics, EV charging, drones, heaters, motors, or industrial machinery. The goal is not to make a prototype look safe; the goal is to stop unsupported product claims until evidence exists.

blocked claim register release-hold gates evidence-driven approval India productization ready

Tool purpose

Use this tool to convert open-source hardware research into a production-grade safety ledger. Every hazard gets an owner, mitigation, required evidence, review status, and release gate.

This is the final gate after source intake, power design, UI state, docking geometry, fault handling, storage integrity, calibration, interlocks, thermal derating, local APIs, QA fixtures, and enclosure DFM.

Use when

  • A prototype may become a kit, supplier package, public demo, paid service, or deployed product.
  • The design contains batteries, mains, motors, heaters, cameras, RF, drones, EV charging, robots, lab measurements, or industrial machines.
  • A marketing or documentation claim could imply certification, safety, medical/diagnostic ability, RF permission, autonomous operation, or field readiness.

Pass / hold / block gates

Pass

Evidence exists, mitigation is implemented, review owner signs off, and the claim is limited to what was actually tested.

Hold

Prototype can remain internal, but cannot be sold, published as production-ready, or operated around users until missing tests are resolved.

Block

The design or claim is unsafe, unsupported, uncertified, legally sensitive, or outside project scope. Do not ship or demonstrate as usable hardware.

Project applicability cards

P01

Electronics research bundle

Role: Foundation study bundle for SiC MOSFETs and embedded Linux

Use: Separate learning claims from production-safe claims before translating theory into hardware.

Hazards: High voltage, fast switching, thermal stress, layout parasitics, shock/fire risk

Release gate: No powered prototype claim without schematic, BOM, derating calculation, test plan, and qualified review.

P02

Air-gapped 8MB module research pack

Role: Offline storage cartridge architecture

Use: Track corruption, brownout, level-shift, flash-write and data-integrity hazards.

Hazards: Silent corruption, partial writes, floating flash pins, brownout, wrong voltage rails

Release gate: No trusted storage claim until power-loss and dual-manifest recovery tests pass.

P03

Air-gapped 8MB CAD implementation bundle

Use: Block fabrication release until ERC/DRC, flash bus, USB power, and enclosure safety are reviewed.

Hazards: Prototype Gerbers, unverified footprints, USB serial misuse, 5V/3.3V damage

Release gate: No PCB order using preliminary outputs; regenerate after netlist/footprint/electrical review.

P04

Compliance checklist

Before supplier review

  • All source files, CAD, BOM, firmware, manuals, and datasheets are pinned and hashed.
  • All user-facing claims have an evidence source or are marked as blocked.
  • Every power, thermal, motion, RF, battery, privacy, and enclosure hazard has a mitigation.
  • Every missing mitigation has a release-hold entry.

Before powered demo

  • Physical emergency stop is tested where motion/heaters/relays are present.
  • Fuse, disconnect, current limit, and brownout behavior are verified where applicable.
  • Firmware watchdog and safe-state behavior are demonstrated.
  • Operator checklist is printed or visible locally.

Before customer or public use

  • Applicable standards and local regulatory obligations are identified.
  • Dangerous unsupported claims are removed from UI, docs, pitch, and labels.
  • QA test receipts exist for the exact hardware revision being used.
  • Maintenance and failure instructions are clear enough for non-engineers.

Blocked claim examples

  • “Flight ready” for the drone bundle without flight safety evidence.
  • “Medical diagnostic microscope” for OpenFlexure without regulatory validation.
  • “Certified EV charger” for OpenEVSE without certified electrical review.
  • “Safe AMR around people” for OpenAMRobot without physical E-stop and mobile robot risk assessment.
