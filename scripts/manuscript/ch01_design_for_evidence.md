# Chapter 1: Designing for Evidence

To understand the leap from software to physical hardware, you have to look at extreme constraints. Let's look at the Winbond W25Q64JV—a tiny, standard 64 Mbit (8 MB) SPI NOR flash chip. 

In a traditional web app, no one cares if a JSON payload has 50 kilobytes of useless, nested padding. But when you are designing an offline carrier to store a deterministic hardware graph for manufacturing, every byte is a premium real estate transaction.

## A Real-Life Manufacturing Edge Case

Let's say you are building a custom drone controller. You design the schematic and send the Bill of Materials (BOM) to the factory. However, the specific 3.3V Linear Dropout Regulator (LDO) you wanted is out of stock. The factory suggests a substitute part. 

You look at the substitute: it outputs 3.3V, it has the same 3 pins, and it costs the same. You approve the substitution. 

When the assembled boards arrive, the drone powers on for exactly 12 seconds before the LDO overheats and shuts down. Why? Because the substitute part had a completely different thermal package (SOT-23 instead of SOT-223). It couldn't dissipate the heat generated by the voltage drop. You just wasted your entire manufacturing budget.

## The Cost of Bloat

Let's look at a quick comparison between a typical web application mindset and the embedded mindset required for an 8 MB constraint:

| Concept | Web Application | Embedded 8 MB Environment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Storage Medium** | Unlimited Cloud RDS | SPI NOR Flash |
| **Capacity** | Terabytes | 8,388,608 Bytes |
| **Missing Data** | Coerced to `null` or `0` | Throws `UNKNOWN` error |
| **Payload Strategy** | Send everything | Canonical JSON, strict stripping |

When you only have 8 MB to capture the absolute physical truth of a system, you cannot afford to guess. This brings us to a strict **design-for-evidence** model. 

1. **Define the Problem**: We need to store a battery pack, its thermal limits, its driver configuration, and all its metadata securely on a tiny flash chip.
2. **Tie Decisions to Physics**: A system must force the builder to input the *exact* thermal package data. It shouldn't let you substitute a part without re-running the physics validation against the new datasheet.

You can't just "fix it in software" if your hardware melts. Builders need tools that enforce honesty, which we'll cover in [Chapter 2: The Honesty Principle](#chapter-2-the-honesty-principle).
